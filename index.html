Planning philosophy: This itinerary is built around comfort, not kilometres. Every day has a slow morning, rest-friendly pacing, and accessible venues. Day trips are handled by private coach or guided tour to eliminate navigation stress for your parents. Activities are selected to minimise steep stairs and long walks.
01
7-Day Itinerary
Day 1
Arrival · Settle In · Hakata Walk
~22–24 Mar
Afternoon
Arrive at Fukuoka Airport (FUK)
Take the subway from the airport to Hakata Station — only 2 stops, about 5 minutes, fully accessible with lifts. Check in to hotel and freshen up.
🚇 5-min subway from airport
Late Afternoon
Canal City Hakata stroll
Fukuoka's iconic open-air shopping mall is fully flat and wheelchair-friendly. Browse shops, grab a coffee, and enjoy the fountain plaza. Light on the legs, easy to navigate.
♿ Fully flat & accessible
Evening
Welcome dinner — Hakata Tonkotsu Ramen
Settle into Fukuoka's famous food culture at a sit-down ramen restaurant near the hotel. Ichiran Hakata has individual booths for privacy and comfort — ideal for elderly diners. Creamy pork bone broth, perfectly seasoned.
🍜 Ichiran or Shin Shin Hakata
Day 2
Dazaifu Shrine & Kyushu National Museum
Easy half-day trip
Morning
Relaxed breakfast at hotel
No early rush. Allow your parents a leisurely start. Depart Hakata Station around 9:30 AM via Nishitetsu Railway for a 40-min comfortable train ride to Dazaifu.
10:30 AM
Dazaifu Tenmangu Shrine
One of Japan's most revered Shinto shrines, dedicated to the deity of scholarship. Walk through serene grounds, cross the three famous bridges (shallow incline, manageable for older guests), and try the beloved Umegae Mochi — grilled rice cakes fresh from roadside stalls. Late March sees the last plum blossoms and early cherry blossoms.
🌸 Plum blossoms still blooming ⛩️ Gentle flat paths
12:30 PM
Lunch on Approach Street (Omotesando)
The tree-lined street approaching the shrine is full of small restaurants and tea houses. Try Chikushi-an for warm udon or soba — light and easy on the stomach.
2:00 PM
Kyushu National Museum
A 5-minute walk (or free escalator tunnel) from the shrine leads to this world-class museum. Fully air-conditioned, with lifts and benches throughout. The permanent gallery covers 5,000 years of Asian and Japanese cultural exchange — captivating for all ages. Allow 1.5–2 hours.
🏛️ Lifts & rest areas throughout
Evening
Return to Hakata · Dinner at Nakasu izakaya
Relax at a traditional Japanese pub serving grilled skewers, tofu, sashimi, and small shared plates. Comfortable seating, warm atmosphere.
Day 3
Ohori Park & Fukuoka Castle Ruins
Relaxed local day
Morning
Ohori Park — Cherry Blossom Walk
Late March is peak cherry blossom season. Ohori Park's 2km lakeside path is flat, paved, and beloved by elderly Japanese walkers. Stroll at your pace, stop on benches, enjoy blossom reflections on the pond. Rent a pedal boat if your parents feel up to it.
🌸 Peak sakura viewing spot ♿ Flat, paved, wheelchair-friendly
11:30 AM
Ohori Park Japanese Garden
Just inside the park is a serene traditional garden with stone lanterns, a tea pavilion, and koi ponds. Considered one of Fukuoka's finest spaces for quiet reflection. Tea ceremony available by reservation (¥500 per person).
🍵 Tea ceremony optional
1:00 PM
Lunch near Ohori — Hakata Mizutaki hotpot
Mizutaki is Fukuoka's famous chicken hot pot — mild, clear broth, tender chicken and vegetables. Perfect comfort food and very gentle on elderly digestive systems. Hakata Mizutaki Hanamidori is a reliable choice nearby.
Afternoon
Rest period at hotel
A mandatory afternoon rest is built into every day for your parents. This is not downtime — it's what makes the rest of the trip enjoyable for them.
😴 Rest hour built in
Evening
Tenjin Yatai Experience
Fukuoka's famous outdoor food stalls line Watanabe-dori in Tenjin from 6 PM. Sit on covered stools at a riverside yatai and eat your way through yakitori, gyoza, and ramen as lantern light flickers on the Naka River. Cash only — bring ¥3,000–4,000 per person.
🏮 Iconic Fukuoka experience
Day 4
Day Trip — Yufuin Onsen Village
Guided coach tour · ~10 hrs
8:00 AM
Guided Coach Departure from Hakata Station
Book a private or small-group guided day tour to Yufuin. This eliminates all driving, navigation, and transit stress for your parents. Coach is air-conditioned, with comfortable seats and scheduled rest stops. Travel time is approximately 2 hours.
🚌 Book: Yufuin Day Tour via Klook or KKday
10:30 AM
Lake Kinrin Stroll, Yufuin
Arrive in the charming mountain spa town of Yufuin. Walk along mist-shrouded Kinrin Lake — flat, beautiful, atmospheric. The steam rising from geothermal vents gives it an otherworldly feel. The main street Yunotsubo Kaido is pedestrian-friendly with artisan shops, local sweets, and cafés.
🌿 Scenic, peaceful, low-exertion
12:00 PM
Lunch in Yufuin
Enjoy a sit-down lunch at one of the town's cosy restaurants — Oita-style wagyu beef sets, fresh tofu, or light kaiseki are all available at moderate prices.
2:00 PM
Foot-bath Onsen (Ashiyu)
Ideal for elderly travellers — a free or low-cost public foot bath (ashiyu) lets you soak feet in natural hot spring water while sitting comfortably on benches. Multiple spots around Yufuin. The warm mineral water is deeply relaxing and excellent for circulation.
♨️ No undressing required — just roll up trousers
3:30 PM
Return Coach to Fukuoka
Relax on the coach ride home. Arrive Hakata by early evening. Light dinner or room service recommended after a full day.
Day 5
Yanagawa Canal Cruise &柳川 Day Trip
Gentle boat ride · Half-day trip
9:30 AM
Nishitetsu Train to Yanagawa
A 40-minute comfortable train ride south of Fukuoka reaches Yanagawa, a historic castle town famous for its network of canals. Direct, easy train route — no transfers required.
10:30 AM
Yanagawa Canal Boat Cruise (Donko Fune)
One of Fukuoka's unmissable experiences — a slow-moving flat-bottomed punt boat glides you through willow-lined canals while a boatman poles and sings folk songs. This is effortlessly gentle — no walking required, just sit and enjoy. Perfect for elderly travellers. Cherry blossoms line the banks in late March. Duration: ~70 minutes.
🛶 ~¥1,600 per person, best activity for elderly 🌸 Sakura lines the canals in late March
12:30 PM
Unagi (Eel) Lunch — Yanagawa Speciality
Yanagawa is famous for its seiro-mushi (steamed eel over rice), one of Japan's most celebrated regional dishes. Restaurants near the boat departure points serve excellent sets. Nutritious, tender, and deeply satisfying.
🍣 Regional must-eat dish
Afternoon
Return & Rest
Back in Hakata by 3 PM. Afternoon rest for your parents. The younger members can explore Tenjin shopping district independently.
Evening
Dinner — Mentaiko & Seafood
Try Fukuoka's other signature — mentaiko (spicy cod roe), fresh Hakata seafood, and warm miso soup. Umi no Nakamichi area restaurants offer fresh Genkai Sea catch.
Day 6
Fukuoka Tower & Momochi Seaside
Leisurely city day
Morning
Free morning — shopping & souvenir hunting
Hakata Station's AMU Plaza basement food floors are perfect for buying Hakata mentaiko, Fukuoka sweets, and speciality gifts. Sit-down café breakfast recommended inside the station.
11:00 AM
Fukuoka Tower
Japan's tallest seaside tower at 234m, fully accessible by lift. The observation deck on the 123m level offers 360° views of the city, Hakata Bay, and — on clear days — the mountains of Kyushu. Beautifully atmospheric in late March light.
🗼 ~¥800 admission · Lift access
12:30 PM
Lunch at Momochi Seaside
The beachfront around Fukuoka Tower has several family restaurants with views of Hakata Bay. Sea breeze, fresh catch, and comfortable seating.
Afternoon
Kushida Shrine & Hakata Traditional Area
Fukuoka's most important shrine, dedicated to the deities who protect the city. Calm, beautiful grounds with ancient camphor trees over 1,000 years old. Easy walking distance from the hotel. The nearby Hakata Machiya Folk Museum tells the story of the city's merchant culture.
⛩️ Flat grounds, benches throughout
Evening
Farewell Dinner — Kaiseki or Motsunabe
Celebrate the trip over a special meal. Motsunabe (offal hot pot in a rich soy-miso broth) is deeply warming and a Fukuoka signature. Or opt for a multi-course Japanese kaiseki at a hotel restaurant for a more elegant send-off.
Day 7
Nanzoin & Morning Markets · Departure
Flexible last day
Morning
Nanzoin Temple — Reclining Buddha
Optional morning visit (if energy allows): the world's largest bronze reclining Buddha lies peacefully in a forested hillside temple 20 minutes from Hakata by JR Sasaguri Line. The temple grounds are quiet and deeply peaceful. Mostly flat walking with gentle inclines.
🙏 Meaningful and calm
Late Morning
Last souvenir run — Hakata Station
Hakata Station's Deitos and Kitte basement malls have everything you need: Hakata ramen kits, mentaiko sets, Fukuoka sweets (Hakata torimon, Niwaka senbei), and travel accessories. Easy to navigate, fully climate-controlled.
Afternoon
Check-out & Airport Transfer
Take the subway from Hakata Station to Fukuoka Airport in 5 minutes. Arrive 2 hours before departure.
🚇 5-min to airport — stress-free departure

04
Elderly-Friendly Travel Tips
🚇
Fukuoka's Best Asset: The Airport
Fukuoka Airport is only 2 subway stops from the city centre — one of the shortest airport-to-city connections in the world. No long transits, no taxing walks with luggage for elderly parents.
♿
Accessibility is Excellent
All Fukuoka subway stations have lifts. The city is compact and walkable. When in doubt, take a taxi — fares are short and reasonable. Avoid rush hour (8–9 AM) on trains.
💴
Cash is Still King
Yatai food stalls, smaller ramen shops, and many shrines are cash-only. Carry ¥5,000–10,000 per person in yen. Get Suica or Fukuoka City IC card for convenient train travel.
🌸
Book Accommodation Early
Late March is cherry blossom season — Fukuoka fills up fast. Book hotels and any day tours at least 3–4 months in advance. Sakura timing varies by year; check JMA forecasts closer to departure.
🏥
Medical Preparedness
Bring sufficient prescription medication with a doctor's letter in English. Fukuoka has excellent hospitals. Consider travel insurance with medical coverage — especially important for your 70s parents.
🍜
Dietary Needs
Japanese food is generally light and healthy. Alert restaurants to any allergies in advance. Most ramen broths are pork-based. Soba, udon, grilled fish, tofu, and steamed dishes are widely available and gentle on older stomachs.
📱
Connectivity
Get a Japan SIM card or pocket WiFi at the airport. Google Maps works excellently in Fukuoka. Download the Fukuoka City tourist app for English guides and maps before departure.
👟
Footwear Matters
Pack comfortable non-slip walking shoes. Many shrine and temple interiors require removing shoes — slip-on shoes make life much easier for elderly visitors. Temple floors can be polished and slippery.

06
Why Fukuoka in March
Fukuoka is arguably Japan's most underrated city — and it's perfect for a multi-generational family trip.

The airport is 5 minutes from the city. The food is extraordinary. The city is compact. The people are warm and welcoming. Late March brings cherry blossoms without Tokyo's overwhelming crowds, and the weather is mild enough for your parents to be comfortable outdoors. Fukuoka doesn't require you to rush. It rewards the slow and the curious — which is exactly how this trip should be.
